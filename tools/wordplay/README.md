# Wordplay thread tools

These are the scripts the Wordplay thread (a Claude cloud session in Shubhanshu's claude.ai project) used to find, write, check and hand over the Layers notes on wordplay, names, allusions and, from round 3, figures of speech, parallelism and character contrasts. They are kept here because CLAUDE.md asks that nothing important live only in a session's container.

They are a record of how the notes were made, not a supported tool. Paths inside them point at the session's scratch folder (`/tmp/claude-0/.../scratchpad/wp`) and at local copies of public-domain commentaries that are not in this repository. No data, source text or quotation store is committed here: the quote rows hold short quotations from copyrighted works (the NET notes, the Reformation Study Bible and others), which may be cited but not copied into the repository.

## How a note is made

1. **Leads.** `mine3.py` mines candidate verses from ten local believing commentaries (Gill, Clarke, Barnes, JFB, Keil and Delitzsch, Henry's concise commentary, Wesley, the Companion Bible, Figures of Speech, the Tyndale open study notes). `r3/figscan.py` and `r3/figcands.py` do the same for Bullinger's figures. A triage workflow keeps the leads worth writing up.
2. **Writing and checking.** `w3/write4.js` (a Workflow script; `w3/write3.js` is the version before the founder decisions of 2026-10-20, kept to resume older runs) writes each note with its cites, then a skeptic, a fresh checker and a second reader check it. `workflows/held-second-writer-*.js` searches every citable work for a second writer for passages held on one writer. `w3/contrast.js` sweeps the narrative books for character contrasts that believing writers state.
3. **Quote checks.** `cq.py` (the local commentaries), `osn.py` (Tyndale study notes), `cat.py` (Catena Aurea) and `vw.py` (BSB wording, Hebrew and Greek) confirm every quotation verbatim before it is kept.
4. **Hand-off files.** `w3/post3.py` and `w3/apply_held.py` turn workflow results into `new-passages.json`, `additions.json`, `quotes.json` and `held-back.json`, re-checking each quote and capping each strength by its distinct writers. `finalize.py` assigns quote rows to notes. The Layers thread merges the files into `config/layers.json`; this thread never edits that file.
5. **True credits (from 2026-10-24, PR #63).** `tc/` carries a hand-off over to the true-credits rules. `tc/resolve.py` mirrors the build's `resolve()` (each cite to its writer by book and chapter), `tc/convert.py` drops cites outside the cite list and recounts strengths by people, `tc/mk_ev.py` and `tc/evidence-true-writers.js` name the true writer in evidence, and `tc/mk_reads.py`, `tc/reads-reread.js`, `tc/reads-retry.js`, `tc/cb-words*.js` and `tc/cb-scan.js` re-read every cite on the page it links, with the time. `tc/collect.py` and `tc/cbfix_collect.py` gather those reads, `tc/assemble.py` writes the hand-off files and a `reads` evidence file, and `tc/layersfmt.py` writes `config/layers.json` in main's own layout so a merge only adds lines.
6. **Figures at Deep (from 2026-10-22).** Figure, parallelism and contrast notes now go in their own files, `config/figures/<OSIS>.json`, in the format the Layers thread set for the `figures-at-deep` build (`extra_figures.rs`). `fig/figwrite.js` writes them (a writer, a skeptic and a second reader, each running the checks), `fig/figcheck.py` runs the build's checks in Python plus this thread's citation rules and checks local quotes word for word, and `fig/figpost.py` turns the workflow results into `config/figures/<OSIS>.json`, `quote-checks.json` and `report.json`. Only works on the `quotable` list in `config/figure-forms.json` are quoted; Wesley is never quoted (cite Henry or Bengel), nor Barnes on Genesis (that text is James G. Murphy's).

## Rules the tools enforce

- Cite only works in `config/layer-sources.json`, and never a name on its avoid list. Church fathers only from New Advent or CCEL translations.
- A writer is a distinct interpreter. Reference works (BSB, Tyndale House, Strong's, Abbott-Smith, Easton) do not count, and Bullinger's two works count once, as do Henry's two.
- A writer counts when he states the point in his own voice on that verse. Views he only reports do not count. From 2026-10-21 (founder decision 9, "The see [quote] is fine"), a pointed cross-reference also counts toward a note that links two passages (quotation, allusion, fulfillment, pattern, contrast): the writer, on this verse, points to the very passage the note links. It never counts toward a wordplay, name meaning, figure or parallelism note, and a note backed only by pointers says no more than that the passages are linked. `w3/pointers.py` finds such references in the local works, `w3/pointer.js` checks them and searches the web commentaries, and `w3/apply_pointers.py` adds what a skeptic kept.
- Strength: 3 or more writers "widely agreed", 2 "commonly held", 1 "some interpreters". Wesley's notes count as Matthew Henry in the Old Testament and as Bengel in the New (his own prefaces say he abridged them).
- Left out because the cited words deny Scripture (founder decisions 2, 20 and 23): A. S. Aglen's volumes of Ellicott (Psalms, Song of Solomon, Obadiah, Jonah); Ellicott, Keil and Delitzsch and the Pulpit Commentary on Ecclesiastes; Calvin on Matthew 27:9 and Acts 7:14 and 16; Delitzsch on Psalms 74:4, 86:1 and 130:1; the Pulpit Commentary on 2 Corinthians 3:7. The condemned and disputed church fathers are left out for now, as the approved voices shortlist says (Origen, Didymus, Tertullian, the Catena's Arian writer on Matthew, and others in `lib3.BARRED_FATHERS`).
- No note, source line or credit mentions AI (founder decision 15). Each quote row records where it was read and the date: for a local copy, the day the hand-off script found the quote again in the pinned file; for a web page, the last day a checking agent fetched that page (`w3/fetchlog.py`), never a guessed date (decision 10). Quotes from modern copyrighted works stay out of the repository; their rows carry a pointer line instead.

`runs.txt` is the thread's run log, with every workflow run id and decision in order.
