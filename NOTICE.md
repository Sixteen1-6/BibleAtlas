The repository does not contain Bible text or source data. `atlas fetch` downloads it from the original projects at the commits pinned in `sources.json`, and `atlas build` converts it.

## Changes made to the STEPBible data

STEPBible asks that changes to its data be noted. The build changes the presentation only, never the words or the tags:

- Verses are renumbered onto the BSB versification. Hebrew words follow the English (NRSV) reference given in TAHOT; Greek words use the KJV reference given in brackets where TAGNT supplies one, because the BSB follows KJV numbering at those points (for example 2 Corinthians 13:12 to 14 and 3 John 1:14 to 15).
- Psalm titles (verse 0 in TAHOT) are attached to verse 1, as English Bibles print them.
- Morpheme separators (`/` and `\`) are removed from the displayed Hebrew and transliteration.
- Lexicon definitions are converted from HTML to plain text segments (bold, italic, line breaks and verse links are kept).
- Words found only in non-base editions are kept and flagged, and are excluded from word counts.
- Each root's uses are grouped by their grammar code into forms (forms/*.json). For Hebrew and Aramaic the form is the root's own part of the word, without prefixes or accents and with the first letter's dagesh as it stands on its own; a form that is only ever used with a pronoun ending is shown with the ending. When a prefix took the first letter's vowel (לֵאמֹר), the vowel the word has on its own is put back (אֱמֹר). Proper-noun and noun kinds that read the same are merged, a number word tagged both as a noun and as a number is counted as whichever the source uses more, and a preposition with the article is counted as a preposition. Where a word's parts don't line up with its grammar code, or a word is joined to a name, that use has no form. For Greek, grave accents are written acute, an accent thrown back by a following enclitic is removed (ἐλέησόν is ἐλέησον), an elided word keeps its apostrophe (ἀλλ᾽), a capital at the start of a sentence is lowered, the title, name and indeclinable marks (-T, -N, -I, -LI) are set aside, and a word the source files under its first part (ἀπέπνιξαν under ἀπό) has no form. Each form shows its most used spelling, as the word stands on its own where it often does (כֹּל, not the כָּל־ joined to the next word) and with the ה a prefix swallows put back on a Hiphil or Niphal infinitive (הַמְרוֹת for the מְרוֹת of לַמְרוֹת); other spellings with at least a tenth of its uses are listed beside it (οὐ, οὐκ), but never half of a name written as two words. A number's gender is read from its spelling (אֶחָד, אַחַת; שָׁלֹשׁ, שְׁלֹשָׁה), since the source tags every אֶחָד feminine. Two hand corrections to the source's grammar: ἐμοῦ is the pronoun "of me" (P-1GS) unless it follows τοῦ, where it is the possessive "my" the source gives every use; and a sense the Hebrew lexicon has no entry for (H0430J) keeps the source's own gloss ("gods"), not the gloss of its number's first entry ("God").
- Word families (forms/*.json) group a root's senses into dictionary words by TBESH and TBESG entries (the same entry, or "a Meaning of" or "a Spelling of" another sense in the same language), name the same word in Hebrew and Aramaic from "in Aramaic of" and "in Hebrew of" where Strong's ties the two numbers or the pair was checked by hand (and not where Strong's tie is only a guess, as שְׁמַשׁ "to serve" and שֶׁמֶשׁ sun), and list "a Form of" as another form of the same word. Names and parts of names are left out.
- Strong's derivation lines give each word the word it comes from. Guesses ("perhaps", "probably", "apparently", "akin to", "compare"), links that rest on a sense the reader can't see ("in the sense of", "through the idea of"), comparisons ("like", "formed like"), collateral forms and compounds of two words are left out, except a Greek word built on a prefix (ἐξέρχομαι on ἔρχομαι). "From the same as", by-forms, "another form for" and a Greek "from the base of" a noun or other word that is not a verb make two words share a root, with neither coming from the other. A Greek word used for another in some tenses (φάγω for ἐσθίω) is listed as another form of it. When one Strong's number covers several words that only look alike (אַיִל ram, pillar, terebinth), the link goes to the one with nearly all the uses or to a sense checked by hand, and otherwise to none. A verb Strong's says is "used only as denominative" from a noun comes from that noun (עָשַׂר to tithe from עֶשֶׂר ten), and the noun's own "from" that verb is dropped. Senses STEPBible files under one number that are other words (קֹל frivolity under קוֹל voice) are kept apart from the number's words. A short hand-checked list in crates/atlas-cli/src/family.rs corrects slips in Strong's numbers and leaves out old guesses that are now given up (כֶּסֶף silver from "to long for").
- Abbott-Smith's notes on Septuagint usage in TBESG are read into a table of Greek and Hebrew root pairs (lxx.json, root numbers only). Hebrew words in those notes are matched to TBESH entries by their spelling; when several entries share a spelling, the verses the note cites or an entry with nearly all the uses decides, and otherwise the word is left out. Phrases, names, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions and particles are left out.

## Christian writers of the past

The writers' notes are copied word for word; only their placing and layout are the app's, and a few marks of the copies are taken out. Each Catena passage is placed on the verses its source keys it to (Douay-Rheims numbering, which starts each passage on the same verse as the English numbering); the four passages that end Matthew 17, Mark 4, Mark 9 and John 11, which that numbering makes one verse shorter, run to the end of the chapter, and the two whose keys stop short of the next passage (Matthew 5:14, Luke 2:22-25) run on to it. The Catena's quotations are shown under the Fathers' names as its source gives them, with "Clement of Alexendria" spelled Clement of Alexandria; its source joins Aquinas' own short links between quotations to the quotation before them. Left out of them: the Gospel's own verses where the copy prints them inside a quotation ("25. And, behold, there was a man"), and the footnote marks it keeps on words ("Hebrewa", "there1"). Henry's notes are placed by his own headings ("Verses 1-9"); text before a chapter's first heading (introductions to books, chapter outlines) is left out, a chapter with no heading is one note on the whole chapter, and six notes that run past their headings (Deuteronomy 20:10, four in Proverbs, John 3:1) cover the verses they discuss; his verse numbers written "#(19)" are shown as "19". Wesley's notes are placed on the verse the module gives; the 29 the extractor put on a "verse 0" are left out, as are the 197 it repeats on the next verse with a note (Psalm 22:31's note again on Psalm 23:1), and a note the module puts on the first of several verses is moved to a later one of them when the words it explains are in that verse of the King James Version and not in the first (31 notes). The verse ranges in the Let's Church files are otherwise not used, because its extractor guesses them. References the modules mark become links where they name verses the Berean Standard Bible has; a bare number after a reference ("1Co 10:1, 2") is a link only when it is in the same chapter. The files built from these (extras/voices.json and extras/voices/) are public domain.

## Writers cited in Layers of meaning

Layers of meaning notes cite Christian writers by name and link to the page where each one's words can be read. The app copies none of their words: for each citation it ships the writer, who he was, the work and its date, where the app read it, the date it was read, and the link. Short quotations kept to check each citation are in docs/evidence/, from public-domain writers only. The Pulpit Commentary is in the public domain in print, but Bible Hub's digital text of it carries BibleSoft's notice: the records made from 2026-10-21 say where on the page it makes the point instead of quoting it, and the 73 quotations of it in docs/evidence/layers-mend-2026-10-20.json are to be replaced with the printed volume's words.

Some sites file a work under one name although other men wrote it. Each citation names the man who wrote the words, read from the printed volume's title or contents page and checked by matching the site's text against a scan of that volume (docs/evidence/credits-2026-10-21.json). Where the date a citation was read was not recorded, it says when it was checked against the source instead (docs/evidence/layers-checks-2026-10-11-to-17.json).

## ESV

The ESV text is never stored in this repository or in the build. The server-side proxy (`server/esv.mjs`) requests one chapter at a time, keeps at most 500 verses in memory, and the page shows Crossway's copyright notice with a link to esv.org, as the ESV API terms require. Use beyond the free terms needs a license from Crossway: https://www.crossway.org/permissions/digital/

## NET Bible

The NET text and notes are never stored in this repository, in the build or in the service worker. The reader asks the publisher's own web service (https://labs.bible.org/api_web_service) for one chapter at a time while someone reads the NET, keeps at most two chapters in memory, and under every NET chapter shows "(NET)", with NET linked to netbible.org, and the acknowledgment https://netbible.com/copyright/ asks of free apps, word for word: "Scripture quoted by permission. Quotations designated (NET) are from the NET Bible® copyright ©1996, 2019 by Biblical Studies Press, L.L.C. http://netbible.com All rights reserved." The permissions cover the text only ("without the NET Bible notes"), so the app never shows the translators' notes: at Study a button links the selected verse to its notes on netbible.org. Other uses need permission from Biblical Studies Press.

## The word-meaning model (potion-base-8M)

Ask the Bible uses potion-base-8M, a Model2Vec model by Minish Lab, only to tell which prepared question someone means. Hugging Face is not reachable from where the data is built, so `.github/workflows/mirror-meaning-model.yml` copies its files unchanged from revision bf8b056651a2c21b8d2565580b8569da283cab23 to the `mirror/potion-base-8M` branch, checking each against the SHA-256 Hugging Face lists for it. The build (`web/scripts/ask-meaning.mjs`) keeps the vector of every one of its 29,528 word pieces, each number rounded to one of 15 levels of that piece's own scale, and writes them to web/public/data/ask/model.bin.

How it was made, as its model card and training-set card say: Minish Lab distilled it from BAAI's bge-base-en-v1.5 (MIT License; Shitao Xiao, Zheng Liu, Peitian Zhang and Niklas Muennighoff, "C-Pack: Packaged Resources To Advance General Chinese Embedding", 2023), using the training set minishlab/tokenlearn-c4-en-bge-base-v1.5: 10 million passages from the train split of AllenAI's C4 web corpus (allenai/c4, released under ODC-BY and bound by Common Crawl's terms of use), each with bge-base-en-v1.5's vector for it. That training set's card names no license of its own. Nothing of bge-base-en-v1.5 or of the training text is used or shipped here, only potion-base-8M's own vectors.
