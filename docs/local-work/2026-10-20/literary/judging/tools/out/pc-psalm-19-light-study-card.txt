Literary devices
SAME THOUGHT TWICE
commonly held
The heavens declare the glory of God;
the skies proclaim the work of His hands.
Day after day they pour forth speech;
night after night they reveal knowledge.
Psalm 19:1, 2

Each second line says the first again in other words: the heavens and the skies, day after day and night after night.

שָׁמַיִם
sha.ma.yim
רָקִיעַ

יוֹם
yom
לַ֫יְלָה
lay.lah

The Hebrew of verse 2 doubles its words: yom le-yom, “day to day”, and laylah le-laylah, “night to night”.

Named by John Calvin and Keil and Delitzsch.

What it is, in Bullinger's words: This is when the lines are parallel in thought, and in the use of synonymous words Figures of Speech Used in the Bible (1898), p. 349

THINGS SPOKEN OF AS PERSONS
commonly held
The heavens declare the glory of God;
Day after day they pour forth speech;
Psalm 19:1, 2

The sky is said to speak. It has no voice (verse 3), yet what God made tells of Him.

Named by Bullinger and John Calvin.

What it is, in Bullinger's words: A figure by which things are represented or spoken of as persons Figures of Speech Used in the Bible (1898), p. 861
