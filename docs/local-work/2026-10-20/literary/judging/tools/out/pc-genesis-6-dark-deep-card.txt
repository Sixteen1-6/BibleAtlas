GOD IN HUMAN TERMS
widely agreed · 7 writers
And the LORD regretted that He had made man on the earth, and He was grieved in His heart.

Scripture tells God's sorrow over man's sin in human words, so that we can understand it.

“Regretted” is nacham, the word 1 Samuel 15:29 uses when it says God does not “change His mind”.

A way of speaking. The feeling is told in human words; it does not belong to God as it does to us. John Calvin, John Gill, Matthew Henry, John Wesley, Joseph Benson and Bullinger
A real grief, in human words. God's love is truly pained by sin, though His purposes do not change. Keil and Delitzsch

What it is, in Bullinger's words: This figure is used of the ascription of human passions, actions, or attributes to God Figures of Speech Used in the Bible (1898), p. 871

Anthropopathism: human feelings spoken of God. Bullinger: Anthropopatheia, “Condescension”. Behind this note: All seven call the words a figure. Where they differ is how far the feeling is like ours; both readings are shown and neither is settled here. Sources: Bullinger's Figures of Speech (1898), Anthropopatheia, or Condescension, p. 882, Matthew Henry, Keil and Delitzsch, John Gill, John Calvin, John Wesley and Joseph Benson on Genesis 6:6.
