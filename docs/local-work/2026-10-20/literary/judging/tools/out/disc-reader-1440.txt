What this mockup shows

Every device is marked in the text itself, where the reader already is, and every mark names the writers it rests on. The five excerpts are stacked so all of them can be seen at once; each keeps its own focus verse (in the app there is one selected verse, the one the map lights). Switch the level, the theme and the width above, or with the app's own controls.

Open a state directly: Simple · Study · Deep · Deep with the writers' words · the commentary notice · phone, Study · night sky · paper. Keys 1, 2 and 3 switch the level.

Rules this follows
The original sources are the point. Every mark names its writers at every level: at Simple the first two names ("From John Calvin, Matthew Henry and 5 more"), at Study every name with the strength, at Deep the full credits. Nothing on the page credits AI, and the checking is described ("checked against the sources on 20 October 2026"), never credited.
Each credit names the person who wrote the words. So Keil and Delitzsch's Psalms and Isaiah are credited to Franz Delitzsch and their Genesis and Samuel to C. F. Keil, and Jamieson, Fausset and Brown's Isaiah to A. R. Fausset. Each credit gives a line on who the writer was, the work, its date (and the translator and year of the English words, for Calvin and the German commentators), where the words were read, and the date they were read.
Simple keeps to the verse. At Simple a mark shows only on the focus verse, and only when it is self-evident (a "but" between two lines) or widely agreed (three or more writers). That is why Psalm 19 shows its lines but no label until Study.
Strength is the count of writers who state the point, each on the cite list; the build script computes it and refuses a label that claims more. Bullinger counts once for his two books; Henry once for his two commentaries. No rabbi, Jewish scholar, critic or avoid-list name appears.
The writers' own words are commentary, so at Deep they wait behind "Show what they wrote", which opens the two-page commentary notice the first time (the words and behaviour of the separate notice design). The credits themselves stay open: they say where things come from.
Scripture is the BSB as the app shows it. Every verse, line and excerpt was checked by script against the app's English (BSB.json at the pinned commit) and the live text shards; the poetry lines follow the pinned BSB USFM and join back to each verse exactly. Genesis 6:6 reads "regretted ... grieved in His heart" in the BSB, so those are the words marked.
The map's language. The mirror's arcs and the pair mark are small arcs like the map's; only the app's colour variables are used, so paper and night both follow.
How the writers' words were checked
All 26 credits are checked by the build script for this mockup (reader-mock/tools/content.py): every quotation was found word for word in the page its credit names. Bible Hub pages were fetched on 20 October 2026 (Henry, Clarke, Gill, Calvin, Benson, Spurgeon, Keil and Delitzsch, Jamieson, Fausset and Brown); Wesley in the app's own live copy of his notes; Bullinger in the Figures of Speech text, with the page found in the archive.org scan of the 1898 printing (pages 350, 357, 359 and 882).
Work dates and translators were read on archive.org title pages today: Calvin's Psalms (James Anderson, 1845) and Genesis (John King, 1847); Delitzsch's Psalms (Francis Bolton, 1871) and Isaiah (James Martin, 1867, the words found in the 1877 printing); Keil's Samuel (James Martin, 1866, the words found in the 1868 printing); and the Portable Commentary (Collins, Glasgow, 1863), whose Job to Malachi is by Fausset and which already holds the Isaiah 6:10 words. The other dates come from the credits study.
What is real here, and what is proposed
Real: the BSB words and lines; the Hebrew (STEPBible TAHOT, from the live text shards); the arc field, link counts, strongest links, centrality and the influence strip (all from the live atlas.bin); the quotations lines (the live extras/quotes.json); every writer's words and every credit fact.
Proposed: the device marks, labels and short notes, and their wording. The other extras lines the app shows under these verses (study notes, themes, places) are left out so the device marks stay in view.
Changes from the research, found while building this
Hannah and Peninnah: 3 writers, not 2. Matthew Henry's full commentary on 1 Samuel 1:1 to 8 states the contrast in his own words: "They had different blessings" and "But they were of different tempers".
Psalm 1:6 stands on Henry, Gill and Delitzsch (Delitzsch: "On the contrary, the way of the ungodly ... perishes"; Gill: "the Lord knows, approves of, loves, and delights in the one; he does not approve of and delight in the other").
Psalm 19:1 to 2: Calvin and Delitzsch (2). Calvin: "The repetition which he makes in the second clause is merely an explanation of the first." Delitzsch calls the second line's verb in verse 2 "the parallel word". Jamieson, Fausset and Brown gloss "firmament" as "another word for heavens"; that glosses a word rather than naming the device, so it is not counted. Bullinger files Psalm 19:1 under personification, not parallelism.
Jamieson, Fausset and Brown's Isaiah words are older than the credits study says: they are in the 1863 Portable Commentary (Collins, Glasgow), not first in 1871.
Bible Hub prints Spurgeon with a change: "from Psalm 1:4 to the end" where the older text reads "from verse 4 to the end", so only the words common to both are quoted.
Questions for the founder
Is "restates, opposes, builds, climax" the right plain wording between lines, or would you rather have whole phrases ("says it again", "turns it around")?
Should a two-writer device such as Psalm 19:1 to 2 wait for Study, as here, or show at Simple when the lines themselves make it plain?
At Simple, is "From John Calvin, Matthew Henry and 5 more" the right size for the source line, or should Simple name every writer as Study does?
On a phone the mirror's outline moves under the verse. Is that acceptable, or should the outline open as its own sheet?
