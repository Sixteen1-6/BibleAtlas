HUMAN WORDS FOR GOD

Scripture speaks of God in human terms so that we can understand Him. Yet He is not a man, that He should change His mind (1 Samuel 15:29).

The verb for “regretted”, נָחַם nacham, comes again in 1 Samuel 15:11, “I regret that I have made Saul king”, and in 15:29, “He is not a man, that He should change His mind”.

SOURCESwidely agreed: 7 writers state it

All 7 say this speaks of God in human terms. They differ on what it shows of God:

As a way of speaking: Calvin, Henry, Gill, Wesley, Benson and Bullinger
As something real in God, told in human words: Keil
John Calvin, Reformer, Geneva. Commentaries on Genesis, translated from the Latin by John King, 1847, on Genesis 6:6. Read on Bible Hub, 20 October 2026.
The repentance which is here ascribed to God does not properly belong to him, but has reference to our understanding of him
Matthew Henry, Presbyterian minister, Chester. An Exposition of the Old and New Testament, 1708 to 1710, on Genesis 6:6. Read on Bible Hub, 20 October 2026.
These are expressions after the manner of men
John Gill, Baptist pastor, London. An Exposition of the Old Testament, 1763 to 1766, on Genesis 6:6. Read on Bible Hub (modernised spelling), 20 October 2026.
This is speaking by an anthropopathy, after the manner of men
John Wesley, founder of Methodism. Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, 1765, on Genesis 6:6. Read in this app’s own copy (Let’s Church, pinned), 20 October 2026.
These are expressions after the manner of men, and must be understood so as not to reflect upon God’s immutability or felicity
Joseph Benson, Methodist preacher. The Holy Bible, with Notes, Critical, Explanatory, and Practical, 1811 to 1818, on Genesis 6:6. Read on Bible Hub, 20 October 2026.
this is spoken of God after the manner of men, by the same figure of speech whereby eyes, ears, hands, and feet are ascribed to God
C. F. Keil, Lutheran Old Testament scholar. Biblical Commentary on the Pentateuch (Keil and Delitzsch), translated from the German by James Martin, 1864 to 1865, on Genesis 6:5 to 7. Read on Bible Hub, 20 October 2026.
The repentance of God is an anthropomorphic expression for the pain of the divine love at the sin of man
E. W. Bullinger, Church of England clergyman. Figures of Speech Used in the Bible, 1898, page 882, on Genesis 6:6: under Anthropopatheia. Read in the 1898 printing (archive.org scan), 20 October 2026.
Sorrow and Grief are attributed to God
