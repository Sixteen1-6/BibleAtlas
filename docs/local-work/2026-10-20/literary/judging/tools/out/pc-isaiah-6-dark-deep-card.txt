Literary devices
MIRROR
widely agreed · 3 writers
Make the hearts of this people calloused;
deafen their ears and close their eyes.
Otherwise they might see with their eyes,
hear with their ears,
understand with their hearts,
and turn and be healed.

Three words go out in one order and come back in the reverse order: hearts, ears, eyes, then eyes, ears, hearts.

In Hebrew the eyes and the ears are the same words both times; the heart is lev, then its fuller form, levav.

What it is, in Bullinger's words: The Repetition of the same Words in an inverse Order (but same Sense) Figures of Speech Used in the Bible (1898), p. 299

Chiasm, or introverted parallelism. Bullinger: Epanodos, “Inversion”. Behind this note: The words return in reverse order in the BSB and in the Hebrew. The BSB's own footnote says the verse is cited in Matthew 13:15, Mark 4:12, John 12:40 and Acts 28:27. Sources: Bullinger's Figures of Speech (1898), Epanodos, or Inversion, p. 299 and Jamieson, Fausset and Brown on Isaiah 6:10; Keil and Delitzsch on Isaiah 6:9–10. Suggest a correction
