SAME THOUGHT TWICE
commonly held · 2 writers
The heavens declare the glory of God;
the skies proclaim the work of His hands.
Day after day they pour forth speech;
night after night they reveal knowledge.
Psalm 19:1, 2

Each second line says the first again in other words: the heavens and the skies, day after day and night after night.

The Hebrew of verse 2 doubles its words: yom le-yom, “day to day”, and laylah le-laylah, “night to night”.

What it is, in Bullinger's words: This is when the lines are parallel in thought, and in the use of synonymous words Figures of Speech Used in the Bible (1898), p. 349

Synonymous parallelism. Bullinger's name for the form: “Synonymous or Gradational”. Behind this note: Calvin says it of verse 1. Keil and Delitzsch call the verb of verse 2's second line “the parallel word” to the verb of its first. Sources: John Calvin on Psalm 19:1; Keil and Delitzsch on Psalm 19:2. Suggest a correction

THINGS SPOKEN OF AS PERSONS
commonly held · 2 writers
The heavens declare the glory of God;
Day after day they pour forth speech;
Psalm 19:1, 2

The sky is said to speak. It has no voice (verse 3), yet what God made tells of Him.

What it is, in Bullinger's words: A figure by which things are represented or spoken of as persons Figures of Speech Used in the Bible (1898), p. 861

Personification. Bullinger: Prosopopoeia. Behind this note: Bullinger lists Psalm 19:1 among things spoken of as persons; Calvin says the psalmist gives the heavens a quality that strictly does not belong to them. Bullinger's note on this verse sends readers to his own book on the stars. The app shows his figure, not that teaching. Sources: Bullinger's Figures of Speech (1898), Prosopopoeia, or Personification (his definition is on p. 861; he lists Psalm 19:1 on p. 865) and John Calvin on Psalm 19:1. Suggest a correction

TWO HALVES THAT ANSWER EACH OTHER
some interpreters · 1 writer
A structure some readers see
In the heavens He has pitched a tent for the sun.
By them indeed Your servant is warned;
Psalm 19:4, 11

Bullinger reads the psalm in four parts: the heavens, then the sun in them; the Scriptures, then God's servant in them.

Bullinger ties the second and fourth parts by one Hebrew word, bahem, “in them”, in verses 4 and 11.

What it is, in Bullinger's words: it is alternate when, of the four members, the first corresponds with the third, and the second with the fourth Figures of Speech Used in the Bible (1898), p. 365

Alternation. Bullinger: an alternate correspondence, A B A′ B′. Behind this note: Bullinger pairs the parts by subject, and ties the second and fourth by the Hebrew word bahem, “in them”. One writer only, so it shows only at Deep, as a structure some readers see. It does not settle what the psalm teaches. Sources: Bullinger's Figures of Speech (1898), Correspondence: Alternation, p. 366 on Psalm 19. Suggest a correction
