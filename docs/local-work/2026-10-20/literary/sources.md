## 2. Bullinger, *Figures of Speech Used in the Bible* (1898)

### The book

- Title page: London, Eyre & Spottiswoode; New York, E. & J. B. Young, 1898. Preface signed "25 Connaught Street, London. November, 1899" in both scans (it came out in parts). About 1,104 pages, five appendixes, seven indexes (Bullinger's own description, *How to Enjoy the Bible*, 1916).
- Public domain in the US (published before 1931; Bullinger died 1913). Princeton's scan is tagged `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`; Cornell's says "There are no known copyright restrictions in the United States on the use of the text."
- Bullinger on the rule: FoS's "Note on Figures in General" says "when the Holy Spirit takes up and uses human words, He does so ... with unerring accuracy" and urges attention to "the words which the Holy Ghost teacheth"; Companion Bible Appendix 6 calls the figures "the Holy Spirit's own marking ... of His own words"; the Companion Bible note on Gen 2:4 says the generations show "no trace of the Elohistic and Jehovistic theories". He holds every word inspired. Already accepted on the cite list (`bullinger-fos`, PR #39).
- How it is organised (Analytical Table of Contents, scan lines 978 to 3027): three Divisions. I. Omission (affecting words: Ellipsis, Zeugma, Asyndeton ...; affecting the sense: Aposiopesis, Meiosis, Tapeinosis ...). II. Addition (affecting words, by repetition: Anaphora ... Parallelism, Correspondence; affecting the sense, "figures of rhetoric": Pleonasm, Hyperbole, Merismos ...). III. Change (of meaning: Enallage, Metonymy, Synecdoche ...; of order: Hyperbaton, Antithesis ...; of application: Simile, Metaphor, Allegory, Prosopopoeia, Anthropopatheia, Erotesis ...). Appendixes A to E (italics in the AV, the genitive, homoeoteleuta, homonyms, the eighteen emendations of the Sopherim). Indexes: I figures by proper name, II by English equivalent, III texts illustrated, IV structures, V subjects, VI Hebrew words, VII Greek words.
- Count of figures: Accordance's product listing says 217 (seen in search results). In the scan's Analytical Table of Contents a "NAME: or, English" pattern finds 194 main headings (OCR misses a few, and sub-forms such as Protozeugma or Chreia are listed under their parents). The e-text has 203 separator-delimited sections. The Companion Bible's Appendix 6 condenses it to "The 181 which follow". Use "about 200" unless the founder wants the 217 figure, which is the publisher's count, not one I reproduced.

### The e-text module, measured (`tools/fosub.py`, `fosseg.py`, `fosstruct.py`)

- Every Bible reference is a link `bcv(book,chapter,verse)` with standard book numbers 1 to 66: 11,386 links, 7,304 distinct starting verses. Ranges keep only the first verse in the link (the visible text says "Isa 1:14-15"), so the end verse has to be read from the link text. Some links are doubled (an artifact), so de-duplicate. Hebrew verse numbers Bullinger gives in brackets stay in the text ("Psa 9:18 (19)").
- Figures are separated by a `--------` paragraph, then a bold heading "Name; or, English". Sub-heads are bold paragraphs; indentation is kept as `margin-left` em values.
- Structures keep their letters, their indentation and the italic mark for the matching member: `<strong>A</strong> 1-4. Thanksgiving.` ... `<strong><em>A</em></strong> 9, 10. Thanksgiving.` 194 blocks with three or more lettered members and at least one italic letter: Correspondence 63, Parallelism 44, Ellipsis 22, Polysyndeton 15, Antimetabole 9, Epanodos 4, others 37.
- Missing: the 7 indexes (so Index IV of Structures and Index III of Texts must come from the scans, where the two-column OCR is jumbled).
- Accuracy: 500 random six-word runs of the module's wording (inside link-free stretches) were looked up in the two 1898 OCR texts: 345 found word for word. Five misses were traced by hand (e.g. "We have already considered this under Metalepsis", "Abraham bought a sepulchre from the sons of Hamor"): each is in the scan, and the miss came from OCR punctuation, hyphenation or italics in the scan, not from the module. The module modernises references ("Matt. xvi. 11, 12" becomes "Mat 16:11-12").
- The 1898 OCR itself uses Roman chapter numbers ("Gen. i. 1"); a regex finds only 6,796 references in the Princeton OCR, so the scans are a check, not the parse source.

### The structures

- Type labels in the OCR of `CompanionBibleOT` (`tools/cbstruct.py`): OT 1,690, NT 622, about 2,300 in all; most common "Alternation", "Division" (not a correspondence; OT 230, NT 80), "Introversion", "Repeated Alternation", "Extended Alternation", "Introversion and Alternation". So roughly 2,000 alternation or introversion structures.
- What the OCR looks like (Gen 2:4-25, `thecompanionbible_202504`): "A | 2. 4, 5-. The earth for man and woman. / B | a | -5. For the ground, no man. ... C[d]s. The Garden. ... A | 24, 35. Man and woman for the earth." Letters, bars and verse numbers are there, but "8" can read "s", "19" can read "is", "25" can read "35", and the italic (matching) letters are not marked. Two OCRs of different scans (Haywood and the 1913 per-book upload) can be collated; every member still needs checking against the BSB verse numbers.
- Character pairs live inside some structures. 1 Sam 1:1 to 4:1, "(Repeated Alternation.)": C "Elkanah and family" / "Elkanah's son, Samuel" (1:1-6, 1:4-2:11, 2:18-21, 2:26, 3:1-10, 3:19-4:1) alternates with D "The sons of Eli" / "Eli and his sons" (1:3, 2:12-17, 2:22-25, 2:27-36, 3:11-18). That is Samuel and Eli's sons, set side by side by the text's own order, in Bullinger's words. The note on 1 Sam 1:2 adds "Hannah = Grace. Peninnah = Pearl. Note the Fig. Antimetabole" (Peninnah had children, Hannah had none, in counterchanged order). Each claimed pair still has to be read off a structure or a note, not assumed.

### Appendix 6, "Figures of Speech"

- "The 181 which follow are arranged in alphabetical order", each with pronunciation, English name, one-line definition and one or two example references: about 200 "or" headings with sub-forms, 332 references in 247 bracketed groups (levendwater HTML, `dl/levend/append6.txt`).
- Its Correspondence entry gives the forms: alternate (simple, extended Psa 72:2-17 and Psa 132, repeated Psa 26, Psa 145, Psa 24), introverted (Gen 43:3-5, Lev 14:51-52). Parallelism: synonymous (Gen 4:23-24, Psa 1:1), antithetic (Prov 10:1), synthetic (Psa 19:7-9), complex alternate (Gen 19:25, Prov 24:19-20), repeated alternation (Isa 65:21-22), extended alternation (Judg 10:17), introversion (Gen 3:19, 2 Chron 32:7-8). Good short definitions for the app, in Bullinger's own words, public domain.

### Bullinger's own stance (both works)

He passes the rule as written: an Anglican clergyman who held every word inspired, secretary of the Trinitarian Bible Society 1867 to 1913, and an opponent of the documentary theory. The founder should still know (Wikipedia, the earlier voices note, and the Companion Bible text): ultradispensationalism ("Bullingerism"; H. A. Ironside called it an "absolutely Satanic perversion of the truth"); the soul's unconsciousness between death and resurrection (the Companion Bible repunctuates Luke 23:43; FoS does not treat that verse); the gap theory at Gen 1:2; a Wednesday crucifixion and four men crucified with Christ; the "Gospel in the stars"; gematria in *Number in Scripture*; he was a member of the Universal Zetetic (flat earth) Society and chaired its meeting at Exeter Hall on 7 March 1905. The early church never ruled on him (he is modern). One nuance: Eusebius (*Church History* 6.37) records Arabians who taught that "the human soul dies and perishes with the body" until the resurrection, and a synod at which they were won back; that is close to Bullinger's view, so it is a judgment call for the founder, not a breach of the rule as written. FoS is mostly grammar and rhetoric, so these views surface far less there than in the Companion Bible notes and appendixes.
