# Church fathers of the first millennium in public-domain English (group notes)

Research date: 2026-10-20. Scope: the Ante-Nicene Fathers (ANF, 1885 to 1896) and Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers (NPNF, 1886 to 1900), the older Oxford Library of the Fathers (LF, 1838 to 1885), and later first-millennium writers (Gregory the Great, Bede). Everything below was fetched and looked at; nothing is guessed. Working files are in `fathers/` next to this note (OCR of scans in `fathers/ocr/`, sampled passages in `fathers/samp/`, scripts in `fscripts/`).

## 1. Short answer

1. The best single source is **HistoricalChristianFaith/Commentaries-Database** on GitHub (pin commit `6a4c2e86187a3e11e79ecf2063ae56b61c6295c2`, 2026-10-18). It already keys the fathers to Bible verses, one TOML file per writer and verse, and its LICENSE dedicates the public-domain material to the public domain. But it mixes four kinds of English, and only one of them is usable: the 19th-century translations. It must be filtered entry by entry (rules in section 4). After filtering, the verified old translations give:
   - **John Chrysostom**, homilies on Matthew, John, Acts, Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians to Philemon, Hebrews (NPNF1 vols 10 to 14): 5,447 entries, 6.67 MB, 5,098 verses with a note; near-complete on 17 New Testament books. Verbatim in 6 of 6 checks.
   - **Augustine**, Tractates on John (John 712 of 879 verses), Ten Homilies on 1 John (90 of 105 verses), and the part of the Expositions on the Psalms that HCF hosts itself (804 Psalm verses). Verbatim in 9 of 9 checks. The other 1,297 Psalm entries come from New Advent and are modernized: drop them or rebuild them from Wikisource.
   - **Gregory the Great**, Morals on the Book of Job (LF 1844 to 1850): Job 1,030 of 1,070 verses, 4.03 MB. Verbatim in 3 of 3 checks.
   - **Cyril of Alexandria**, Commentary on John (LF 1874 and 1885): John 703 of 879 verses, 2.70 MB. Verbatim in 3 of 3 checks, except that HCF changed "shew" to "show".
   - Irenaeus, Against Heresies, and Justin Martyr, Dialogue with Trypho (ANF 1): verbatim (5 of 5 checks), but these are arguments, not commentaries; optional.
2. Outside HCF:
   - **Cyril of Alexandria, Commentary on Luke** (R. Payne Smith, 1859): tertullian.org, public domain by Roger Pearse's statement. Sermons 1 to 65 (Luke 1:1 to 10:21) are the 1859 wording. Sermons 66 to 156 (Luke 10:22 to 24:45) were modernized by Pearse in 2008. For the 1859 wording throughout, use the archive.org scans.
   - **Bede, Explanation of the Apocalypse** (Edward Marshall, 1878): only an archive.org scan with OCR, plus an unfinished Wikisource transcription. HCF's "Bede on Revelation" is a different, new translation.
   - **Augustine, Sermon on the Mount and Harmony of the Gospels** (NPNF1 6, 1888): Wikisource or the scans. HCF has only modernized ACCS versions of these.
3. Jerome, Ambrose, Hilary, Ephrem and most of Bede have **no public-domain English translation of their Bible commentaries**. The Catena Aurea already holds their Gospel comments, translated in the Oxford edition of 1841 to 1845. The HCF entries for these writers are either ACCS excerpts (copyrighted) or new unsigned translations. HCF's documented translation process for those uses an AI model.
4. Founder-rule flags for the shipping Catena data, found while checking (section 6): it already quotes **Origen 638 times**, the **Arian author of the Opus Imperfectum 439 times** (labelled "Pseudo-Chrysostom", in Matthew), **Eusebius 82 times** and **Didymus 9 times**.

## 3. What HCF's entries really are (provenance check)

Each HCF entry has `quote`, `source_title`, `source_url` and sometimes `append_to_author_name`. I dumped all 34,441 entries for the 14 fathers in this group (`fathers/hcf_dump.jsonl`) and sorted them by origin (`fscripts/fclass.py`):

| Kind | How to spot it | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 19th-century translation (NPNF, ANF, LF) | `source_url` points to `historicalchristian.faith/by_father.php` in a folder that holds an old translation (list in section 4), and the title is in mixed case ("Homily on the Gospel of Matthew 75") | Use |
| New Advent revision | `source_url` on newadvent.org | Drop. Checked on 3 Augustine Psalm passages: "hath" printed as "has", "meaneth thy word" as "means your word", "ye" as "you", and references such as "[Psalm 97:8]" and "[Acts 10:47]" added. |
| ACCS excerpts | `source_title` in capitals ("SERMON 196.1", "COMMENTARY ON LUKE, HOMILY 12", "HARMONY OF THE GOSPELS 3.21.58") | Drop: copyrighted modern or modernized translations (InterVarsity's Ancient Christian Commentary on Scripture) that HCF keeps under fair use. Checked: Cyril on Luke Hom. 12 in this group reads "all eyes focused on Jesus, wondering perhaps how he could read". Augustine's Sermon on the Mount entries match NPNF only 47% to 99%, with "thy" changed to "your". |
| New unsigned translations | `by_father` folders with no old English translation behind them: all of Bede, Gregory's Forty Gospel Homilies, Homilies on Ezekiel and Commentary on 1 Kings, Ambrose on Luke and on the Psalms, Augustine's "Sermons" folder, all of Jerome's commentaries | Drop. Bede on Revelation says "Translated from Migne's Patrologia Latina" and names no translator. Commits since 2025 say "import fresh translation". HCF's Translation-Tools README calls its process "An LLM-assisted workflow for producing fresh, public-domain English translations". These are not the fathers' words in a published human translation. |
| Catena duplicates | `append_to_author_name = " (as quoted by Aquinas, AD 1274)"` | Drop (already in the app's Catena) |
| Other modern books | links to books.google.com or archive.org, or no link | Drop. For example, Hilary's "Commentary on Matthew" links Google Books id 2398ZZ9dIQoC, which is D. H. ## 4. Filter for an HCF import (proposal)

Keep an entry only when all of these hold:
1. `author` is on the founder-approved list (section 5);
2. `append_to_author_name` is empty (this drops the Catena copies and Irenaeus' 24 entries marked "(Reporting Valentinian Views)");
3. `source_title` is not mostly capital letters (this drops ACCS);
4. `source_url` is a `historicalchristian.faith/by_father.php` link whose folder is one of: Augustine `ON THE PSALMS`, `TRACTATES ON JOHN`, `Ten Homilies on the First Epistle of John`, `The City of God`, `On The Trinity`, `Confessions`; Chrysostom `Homilies/...`; Gregory `Morals on the Book of Job`; Cyril `Commentary on the Gospel of John`, `Commentary on the Gospel of Luke` (if the modernized later sermons are accepted); Irenaeus `Irenaeus Against Heresies`; Justin `Dialogue with Trypho`; Basil `The Hexaemeron`; Gregory of Nyssa `On the Making of Man`, `Against Eunomius`; Hilary `On the Trinity`, `Homilies on the Psalms`; Ambrose's NPNF2 10 folders.

Verse keys come from the file name (`Matthew 24_4.toml`, `1 Kings 19_10-20_3.toml`); book names follow HCF's `book_names.json`. The keys are HCF's choice, and a quote can be keyed to a verse it only cites (for example, Chrysostom on Matthew keyed to 1 Timothy 2:9). Prefer entries whose key is in the book the work comments on, or mark the others "cited". Entries are excerpts chosen by HCF, not whole homilies; where HCF skips text it sometimes leaves no ellipsis (seen in Tractate 83).

## 5. Work by work

Spot checks compared the transcription letter by letter against the archive.org OCR of the printed book (`fscripts/fcmp4.py`). Where the script reported gaps, I looked at the OCR by hand. All the gaps were two-column OCR mixing or OCR noise ("in) mail deceive yon" for "no man deceive you") unless stated otherwise.

### Recommended

**John Chrysostom, Homilies on Matthew, John, Acts, Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, 1 and 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon, Hebrews.** NPNF series I vols 10 to 14 (New York: Christian Literature Co., 1888 to 1890), revising the LF translations (1839 to 1877). Translators, for example: Matthew by Sir George Prevost (LF), revised by M. B. Riddle; Acts by J. Walker, J. Sheppard and H. Browne, revised by George B. Stevens; Romans by J. B. Morris and W. H. Simcox. Public domain in the US. Founder rule: Chrysostom is honoured as a saint and doctor by Orthodox and Catholics and read by Protestants; he is already on the cite list (`chrysostom`). Source: HCF, filtered. Coverage: 5,098 verses in 39 books. Matthew 1005/1071, Acts 961/1007, John 735/879, Romans 431/433, 1 Corinthians 419/437, 2 Corinthians 257/257, Hebrews 301/303, Galatians 148/149, Ephesians 155/155, Philippians 103/104, Colossians 95/95, 1 Thessalonians 89/89, 2 Thessalonians 46/47, 1 Timothy 113/113, 2 Timothy 78/83, Titus 44/46, Philemon 25/25. Size: 6.67 MB. Checks: Matthew Homilies 48, 69 and 75 against NPNF1 vol 10 (archive.org `aselectlibrary10unknuoft`); Romans Homilies 5 and 26 and Acts Homily 44 against vol 11 (`aselectlibrary11unknuoft`). All verbatim; HCF writes Greek words in Latin letters (diamarturasthai). Concerns: the homilies end with long moral exhortations, which HCF keys loosely; the Catena already gives short Chrysostom pieces on Matthew and John in a different translation (Aquinas' Latin put into English), so the same thought can appear twice.

**Augustine, Tractates on the Gospel of John.** Translated by John Gibb (Tractates 1 to 37) and James Innes (38 to 124) for Dods' Edinburgh edition, 1873; printed in NPNF1 vol 7, 1888. Public domain. Founder rule: Augustine is the Western church's chief doctor, honoured by Catholics and Protestants; the Orthodox honour him as a saint while questioning some of his teaching. Source: HCF folder `TRACTATES ON JOHN` (959 entries, 1.46 MB). Coverage: John 712/879. Checks: Tractates 16, 83 and 114 against NPNF1 vol 7 (`aselectlibrary07unknuoft`): verbatim, except one HCF slip, "thou shall" where the print has "thou shalt".

**Augustine, Ten Homilies on the First Epistle of John.** Translated by H. Browne (LF 1848 to 1849), revised by J. H. Myers for NPNF1 vol 7, 1888. Public domain. Source: HCF (190 entries, 0.20 MB). Coverage: 1 John 90/105. Checks: Homilies 3, 6 and 9: verbatim.

**Augustine, Expositions on the Psalms.** NPNF1 vol 8 (1888) is not the full work: its title page says "condensed from the six volumes of the Oxford translation" by A. Cleveland Coxe. The Oxford translation (LF, 6 vols, 1847 to 1857) was by J. E. Tweed, T. Scratton, H. M. Wilkins and others. Both are public domain. Sources: (a) HCF entries hosted in its `ON THE PSALMS` folder: 746 entries, 1.05 MB, 804 Psalm verses, verbatim (checks on Ps 35:18, 41:9 and 119:74 against `aselectlibrary08unknuoft`); (b) the other 1,297 HCF entries come from New Advent and are modernized (checks on Ps 97:8, 119:105 and 145:20). Replace them from Wikisource (NPNF1 vol VIII, one page per Psalm, CC BY-SA) through a mirror. With both parts, HCF covers 2,203 of 2,461 Psalm verses. (c) The full Oxford text exists only as scans (archive.org `expositionsonboo01auguuoft` and others). Concern: Augustine reads the Psalms as the voice of Christ and the Church. That is his method and fits the rule, but it is not a literal reading.

**Gregory the Great, Morals on the Book of Job.** Library of the Fathers vols 18, 21, 23 and 31 (Oxford: Parker, 1844 to 1850). The archive.org record notes "Translation of Text by James Bliss" for vol 3 part 2, and prefaces are signed "C.M." (Charles Marriott). Public domain. Founder rule: Gregory, pope 590 to 604, is honoured as a saint and doctor by Catholics and Orthodox, and his Job is the classic patristic Job. Source: HCF folder `Morals on the Book of Job/GregoryMoralia` (1,151 entries, 4.03 MB), taken from Lectionary Central's transcription. Coverage: Job 1,030/1,070 (96%). Checks: Book 24 (Job 34:12), Book 27 (Job 36:29-30) and Book 28 (Job 38:4) against the LF vol 3 scan (`morals_on_the_book_of_job_1847-s_gregory_the_great`, Public Domain Mark): verbatim. The transcription adds the margin references inline ("[Job 23]", "[Mal. 3, 1]") and keeps "shew". Concerns: Lectionary Central states no license, though the text is public domain; Gregory gives each verse a historical, an allegorical and a moral sense, and readers should be told that the allegory is his.

**Cyril of Alexandria, Commentary on the Gospel of John.** LF vol 43 (1874, P. E. Pusey) and vol 48 (1885, Thomas Randell). Public domain. Founder rule: Cyril was the champion of the Council of Ephesus (431); he is a saint and doctor for Orthodox and Catholics. Sources: tertullian.org (Pearse transcription, public domain, keeps the 1874 spelling) or HCF (922 entries, 2.70 MB; it changes most "shew" to "show": 53 left against 746 changed). Coverage: John 703/879 (80%); Books 7 and 8 (about John 10:18 to 12:48) are lost and survive only as fragments whose authenticity Pearse, citing Quasten, calls questionable. Checks: Books 2, 3 and 4 (John 4:14, 6:16, 6:59) against the 1874 scan (`commentaryongosp01cyri`): verbatim apart from shew/show. Concern: Pusey's English is stiff (Liddon called it "quaint").

**Cyril of Alexandria, Commentary on Luke.** R. Payne Smith, *A Commentary upon the Gospel according to S. Luke*, Oxford University Press, 1859, 2 vols, translated from the Syriac and filled out from the Greek catenas. Public domain. Sources: (a) tertullian.org, 14 pages, 156 sermons, about 1.48 MB of text, public domain by Pearse's statement. Parts 1 to 6 (sermons 1 to 65, Luke 1:1 to 10:21) are the 1859 wording, verbatim in checks on sermons 26-38 and 47-56. Parts 7 to 14 (sermons 66 to 156, Luke 10:22 to 24:45) are Pearse's 2008 modernization; his own preface says "I decided to modernise the text in the remainder of the sermons". Checks on sermons 66-80, 99-109, 102 and 151 show "ye" printed as "you", "resisteth" as "resists" and "sheweth" as "shows", with the wording otherwise kept. (b) The scans `commentaryluke01cyriuoft` and `commentaryuponth02cyriuoft` (about 1 MB OCR each, NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT) for the 1859 wording throughout. HCF has only 91 of Pearse's entries plus 333 ACCS entries (drop). Coverage: Luke 1 to 24 with gaps where the Syriac is lost. Decision for the founder: accept Pearse's modernized half (labelled as such), or OCR the 1859 volumes for the later sermons.

**Bede, The Explanation of the Apocalypse.** Translated by Edward Marshall (Oxford and London: James Parker, 1878). Public domain. Founder rule: Bede is a saint and doctor; no one questioned his orthodoxy. He openly uses the seven rules of Tyconius, a Donatist, and says so. Sources: archive.org `explanationapoc00bedegoog` (Google scan, NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT; OCR 0.36 MB, about 57,000 words); Wikisource `Index:Bede's Explanation of the Apocalypse, translated by E. Marshall.djvu` (not proofread, incomplete). Coverage: all 22 chapters, lemma by lemma with verse numbers ("V.1. angel."). Checks: the OCR reads cleanly with fixable errors ("die good" for "the good", "€ui" for "an", "fetce" for "face"). HCF's Bede on Revelation is not Marshall: letter match 0.66 to 0.78 against the 1878 print in 2 of 3 samples, and no match in the third. Concern: needs OCR cleanup and a proofread; lower priority.

**Augustine, Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount, and The Harmony of the Gospels.** NPNF1 vol 6, 1888 (Sermon on the Mount by William Findlay, revised by D. S. Schaff; Harmony by S. D. F. Salmond, revised by M. B. Riddle; both from Dods' Edinburgh edition of 1873). Public domain. Sources: Wikisource NPNF1 vol VI, or the scan `aselectlibrary06unknuoft` (3.8 MB OCR). HCF has only ACCS versions (drop). Coverage: Matthew 5 to 7 phrase by phrase; the Harmony works through all four Gospels' order. Size: about 1 MB of text together (estimate from the volume). Checks: HCF's ACCS entries against NPNF1 vol 6: 0.47, 0.99 and 0.94 letter match, with "thy" made "your" and "it could have been the case" for the 1888 wording. These checks show why they must be dropped; the Wikisource text was not checked.

### Optional (orthodox, public domain, but not verse commentaries)

- **Irenaeus, Against Heresies** (ANF 1, 1885; Alexander Roberts and W. H. Rambaut, from the Edinburgh Ante-Nicene Christian Library, 1868 to 1869). ### Nothing public domain to add

- **Jerome**: none of his Bible commentaries was put into English before 1930. The Commentary on Daniel is Gleason Archer, 1958. NPNF2 6 (1893, W. H. Fremantle) has only letters and treatises. The Catena already quotes Jerome on Matthew 849 times. HCF's Jerome commentaries are new unsigned translations made in 2025 and 2026.
- **Ambrose**: NPNF2 10 (1896, H. de Romestin and others) has treatises and letters, no commentary. The Catena quotes his Luke 534 times.
- **Hilary on Matthew**: first English was D. H. Williams, 2012 (copyrighted). The Catena quotes it 344 times.
- **Ephrem**: NPNF2 13 (1898, J. B. Morris and A. E. Johnston) has hymns and homilies, no commentary. His commentaries on the Diatessaron and on Genesis exist in English only from the 1990s.
- **Bede's other commentaries** (Luke, Mark, Acts, Catholic Epistles, Genesis, Samuel, Proverbs): English only since the 1980s. HCF's are new unsigned translations.
- **Gregory the Great's Gospel and Ezekiel homilies**: English only since 1990 and 2008. HCF's are new.

HCF's own `condemned_by_council = true` flag is set only for Arius, Pelagius, Novatian, Heracleon, Valentinus, Julian of Eclanum, Tatian and Apollinaris. It does not flag Origen, Theodore, Didymus or Evagrius, so the founder's list must be our own.

## 7. The Catena Aurea already shipped, and what more fathers would add

Catena data (AlvaroBalbin/catena at efe1bd08, 12,692 quotations, 5.37 MB of quoted text) covers every passage of the four Gospels. Quotes by writer: Chrysostom 2,120; Augustine 2,003; Bede 1,263; Theophylact 1,036 (11th century); Jerome 870; Origen 638; Ambrose 585; Pseudo-Chrysostom 560; Gregory the Great 529; Hilary 430; Gloss 422; Cyril of Alexandria 389; Remigius 388; Rabanus 299; Pseudo-Jerome 228; Alcuin 173; Basil 122; Gregory of Nyssa 87; Eusebius 82; Titus of Bostra 82; Athanasius 44.

What adding more would bring:
- **New books.** The Catena stops at the Gospels. Chrysostom gives near-complete Acts, Romans to Philemon and Hebrews; Augustine gives the Psalms and 1 John; Gregory gives Job; Bede (scan) gives Revelation. None of this is in the app now.
- **Whole arguments on the Gospels.** The Catena cuts each father into a few sentences, through Aquinas' Latin. Chrysostom's full homilies on Matthew (1.5 MB) and John, Augustine's 124 tractates on John (1.46 MB), and Cyril on John (2.7 MB; the Catena has only 1 Cyril quote on John) and on Luke give the full reasoning, in translations made from the Greek or Latin originals.
- **Overlap to manage.** The same Chrysostom or Augustine sentence will appear twice, once from the Catena and once from the homily, in different English. Show the homily as the father's own voice and keep the Catena as the chain, or hide a Catena quote when the same father's full text covers that verse.
- **A cleaner Catena text exists.** lemtom/catena (CC0-1.0; the CrossWire "Catena" module, "checked against scans of the original publication"; one 6.6 MB OSIS file, commit aebb0f6b) could be used to cross-check the current copy, which was transcribed from ecatholic2000.com.

## 9. Method notes

- `fscripts/hcf_dump.py` reads the TOML through `git cat-file --batch` (reading 30,000 small files from disk on this Windows machine took over 10 minutes; through git it took 12 s).
- `fscripts/fclass.py` sorts entries by origin; `fscripts/fcov.py` counts covered verses against KJV verse counts (`fathers/kjv/verse_counts.json`, from scrollmapper/bible_databases).
- `fscripts/fcmp4.py` compares a passage with the OCR letter by letter (spacing, hyphenation and punctuation ignored). OCR files are in `fathers/ocr/` (all under 5 MB; nothing over 50 MB was downloaded).
- Not done: a full-text check of the Wikisource NPNF pages against scans (Wikisource marks them 50%, unproofread); finding the GitHub home of the Leipzig CC BY-SA TEI.
