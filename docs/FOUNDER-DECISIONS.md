# Founder decisions on the voices plan, 2026-10-20

Answers to the questions in VOICES-PLAN-LUTHER-FATHERS.md section 7, in the founder's words where quoted.

1. **Luther's harsh words: show them word for word.** "Leave everything Luther said word for word." New rule for all commentary: "the first time viewers look at commentary, any commentary, just give a disclaimer that it's a commentary and not God's word. Make this disclaimer very big and easy to understand and make sure they have to click like 2 pages, not just click okay once."
2. **Passages that deny Scripture are left out.** "Leave Luther passages out that deny scripture; commentaries are still okay." So Luther on 1 and 2 Peter and Jude is used, without the passages saying James is "no true Apostolic Epistle" and Jude was not written by "one who was truly an Apostle"; the same applies to his Prefaces (James, Jude, Hebrews, Revelation, Ecclesiastes) and to any passage in any writer that denies Scripture.
3. **Credit the original source, and use it where possible.** "Also credit the original source. Is there an API, can we use the original source somehow?" Every father's entry names the original translation (series, translator, volume, page or homily) as well as Historical Christian Faith where its repository is used. Open question being researched: taking the words straight from the original 19th-century volumes (archive.org scans and OCR, which have an API; Wikisource, which has an API and is CC BY-SA but not fully proofread).
4. **Cite exactly as the commentary shows it.** "Just cite exactly how the commentary shows." The words appear exactly as the source has them, with an exact citation. Where a compiled excerpt leaves words out of the original, that is shown honestly (preferably by using the whole original passage instead, per decision 3).
5. **Two voices for one father: show both.** "If more than one commentary exists it's fine to have both; there's clearly a distinction by authors." Each entry names its writer and work, so the Catena's quotation of Chrysostom and Chrysostom's own homily stand side by side, labelled.

7. **Old spelling: keep it, and explain where readers would be confused, with a source.** "Tearm / others: let's try to make the Puritan thing cite something that translates it correctly where readers would get confused." Show the Puritan texts as printed; where a spelling or an old word would confuse a reader, show the modern reading beside it, taken from a cited source (candidates being checked: EarlyPrint's standardized spellings for the same EEBO-TCP texts; Webster's 1828 dictionary for old meanings), never invented.

8. **Very long notes: show the opening, with "Read all".** "Opening and read all is allowed."

**The lists: approved.** "I approve the list as long as it respects scripture, like if Luther is giving a commentary on it even Luther's green, but your list looks good." Read as: the shortlists (VOICES-SHORTLIST.md first and second batch, and the fathers list) are approved, every passage must respect Scripture (a passage that denies Scripture is left out, whoever wrote it), and Luther is approved wherever he is commenting on Scripture. The shortlist's recommended answers are taken as approved where he did not answer separately, and he can overrule any of them: start with the shared build change and the first batch (decision 1); fathers Chrysostom, Augustine, Gregory the Great and Cyril of Alexandria now, the condemned kept out, the disputed left out for now, Gregory's allegory labelled "spiritual reading" (4); in the Catena, hide Origen, Didymus and the Arian writer with a visible line that a quotation was left out, keep Eusebius with a one-line note (5); Spurgeon waits for Project Gutenberg's proofread volumes (8); Keil and Delitzsch and the Pulpit Commentary not on Ecclesiastes (9); Gill stays cited only (10); mirror branches for the CrossWire, tertullian.org and Gutenberg sources and one library to read CrossWire's compressed files, Wikisource-derived files credited and marked CC BY-SA (11); the avoid list governs whom the app cites, not names inside an old writer's own words (12); many writers on a verse: listed by name, each opened by the reader and remembered (13). Henry: both the full commentary and the Concise (decision 5 above, which overrides the shortlist's "instead of").

Still open from the Luther plan's list: 6 (which fathers, and the Catena's Origen, Arian author, Eusebius and Didymus quotes; now answered by the shortlist's recommendation), 8 (very long notes), 9 (allegory), 10 (rabbis named inside old writers' own words), 11 (cite list additions), 12 (many writers on one verse), 13 (mirror branches), 14 (Graebner's abridged Galatians).

## Later the same day: credits and counting (Layers mend review)

9. **Cross-references count.** "The see [quote] is fine": a writer's pointed cross-reference such as "See Gen. 3. 19" counts toward a note's strength, as the checkers accepted.
10. **Credit the original writer, where the text was taken from, and both dates.** "The credit should be the original writer and where we pulled the work from. The original timestamp and the truly retrieved timestamp should both be there." Every citation names the person who actually wrote the words (so Bible Hub's "Barnes' Notes" on Genesis is credited to James G. Murphy, its Minor Prophets to E. B. Pusey, and so on), the edition or site it was read from, the original publication date, and the date it was retrieved. This applies to Layers notes and to every writer shown at Deep.
11. **Cite everything, and make every citation help.** "Let's make sure we cite everything and basically all citations really help the reader understand." Every statement the app shows that is not the Bible's own words names its source; a credit tells a first-time reader who the writer was, when, what kind of work, and links to the page to check it. The attribution evidence is kept in the repository so anyone can re-check it.
12. **Nothing that opens empty.** "In the app we can click 'Christian writers' and click a writer and it appears empty ... it should only appear for verses they wrote on so you can't click anything that's empty. That goes for any empty selection." Every line, writer, tab, chip or card a reader can click must open to something; the build should guarantee it (run wf_e5046b06-4ad, branch no-empty-selections).
13. **AI may write the summaries, never the writers' words, and nothing may suggest the commentaries are AI.** First: "The sources and 'Drafted with AI help (Claude) ... Not yet reviewed by a person' shouldn't be included. The sources are the sources. We aren't using AI to do sources or commentaries." Then, clarifying: "the summary can be AI but not the copy paste lines; we do not want to give the impression that these commentaries and stuff is made by AI." So: the app's own short summaries (the Layers note text, and similar app-written explanations) may be written with AI help and may say so, but only on the summary itself; the writers' own words (quotes, the Christian writers at Deep, study notes, dictionary entries) and every source credit carry no AI wording at all, and no line may sit where it reads as if the commentaries or sources were made by AI. Today's Layers passage line ("Drafted with AI help (Claude) from the BSB, STEPBible ... and older commentaries named under each note") mixes the two and must be split: the sources named plainly as sources, and any AI note attached only to the summary. Remove "Not yet reviewed by a person" and the hard-verses and hard-passages AI wording where it touches sources; update CLAUDE.md and docs/HANDOFF.md, whose rule "Disclose AI drafting" this replaces.
14. **Ask the Bible: no per-answer AI line.** "For this it should not be Claude; our disclosure already cites math, algorithm and AI in general and I think that is good enough for this citation, as long as its deterministic part is traceable and the real undeterminism goes to AI." So: no "Verses chosen and ordered for this question with AI help (Claude)" line under each chain; the founder's note (AskSheet.tsx line 414: "It finds verses by math ... even AI and algorithms") is the disclosure. OPEN, asked the founder: the note says verses are found "by math", but the prepared chains were chosen and ordered with AI; does the note keep its words, or gain one clause saying the prepared answers were chosen with AI help?

## The citation audit (CITATION-AUDIT.md), answered 2026-10-20 evening

15. **No AI credit anywhere (audit question 1: no).** "Just credit original source. The whole point is for us to be looking at original sources and this helps just compile them better. No crediting of AI." This settles decision 13's open point: summaries carry no AI line either. Every note must therefore read as what its named sources say, with the sources' own words one tap away.
16. **Ask the Bible stays as it is (audit question 10: "don't do this").** No AI line under chains, "People ask" unchanged, the founder's note keeps its words ("It finds verses by math ... even AI and algorithms"); this also answers decision 14's open point (keep the note as is).
17. **Themes come from outside trusted sources (audit question 14).** "Get general themes we get from the Bible from an outside trusted source; all those themes should already have sources that define them; use them or cite different authors and writers." App-written theme descriptions are replaced by definitions from trusted, credited sources (for example the Bible dictionaries and topical works already shipped, and other believing writers), cited to their original authors. "That's where I am talking about crediting original sources, in response to that question, but it goes for everything."
18. **Every other audit question: the recommended answer** (questions 2 to 9, 11 to 13, 15 to 17), except where it would credit AI (question 11's "checked by AI reviewers": raised with the founder as a conflict).
19. **Checking is described, never credited (settles audit question 11).** "Describe what was done ('checked against the source') and never say who or what checked it; only use 'reviewed' when a person reviewed it." So wherever readers see that a note or citation was verified, the words are "checked against the source" (with the date where known); "reviewed" appears only when a named person reviewed it.
20. **Contributors: keep what respects Scripture, leave out what denies it.** "Keep a contributor's citations that respect Scripture and leave out only passages that deny it. Drop a contributor entirely only when he denies Scripture in the passages we'd cite (like Aglen calling Jonah possibly fiction)." So E. H. Plumptre's 88 citations stay where his cited words respect Scripture; any cited passage of his that denies Scripture goes. A. S. Aglen's citations go (he calls Jonah possibly fiction in the very notes cited). Each contributor's cited passages are judged on their own words.

21. **Hebrew definitions stay, credited truthfully (settles plan conflict 6).** "Keep the Hebrew definitions and credit them truthfully as abridged from Brown-Driver-Briggs (1906). It's a dictionary of word meanings, not commentary." The avoid list keeps governing whom the app cites as an authority; a lexicon's word meanings are credited for what they are. Engineering note decided by recommendation: the extras' "five one-line insertions in shared files" limit was written for parallel extras; the dataset-dates module (AF-DT) may change the shared build code, reviewed like any other change (plan conflict 12).
22. **Word study: Strong's first, then the fuller lexicon.** "Show Strong's own short definition first, credited to James Strong (1890), then the fuller Brown-Driver-Briggs (Hebrew) or Abbott-Smith (Greek) entry, each credited." Strong's definitions are already in the data (sources.json "strongs", Open Scriptures JSON, CC BY-SA); the lexicon module AF-LX of AUDIT-FIXES-PLAN.md now also shows them.
