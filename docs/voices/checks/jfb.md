# Scripture check: Jamieson, Fausset and Brown

What was checked, how, and what was decided, for `config/voices/jfb.json` (decision 23, rules 3 to 5). The notes are from Robert Jamieson, A. R. Fausset and David Brown's *A Commentary, Critical and Explanatory, on the Whole Bible* (1871), as CrossWire's JFB module 3.0 keeps it. Jamieson wrote Genesis to Esther, Fausset Job to Malachi and 1 Corinthians to Revelation, and Brown Matthew to Romans (docs/evidence/credits-2026-10-21.json).

## Method

The notes as the reader keeps them (each book's introduction and the "CHAPTER N" labels are cut) were scanned with the patterns of section 2.5 of docs/local-work/2026-10-20/voices/VOICES-BUILD-SPEC.md: authorship ("not written by", "another hand", "spurious", "interpolat" and the rest), canon ("not canonical", "apocryphal", "straw"), error words ("error", "mistake", "crept in", "amend", "corrupt", "contradict" and the rest) within 60 characters of a word for the text or its writer, and "myth", "legend", "fable". JFB also notes, far more often than Henry, which words the oldest manuscripts have or omit, so a second scan took every paragraph that says a manuscript, a version or "the oldest" omits, adds or reads a word. The two scans flagged 362 paragraphs (253 and 109). Every one of them was read in full against the rules below, next to what the Berean Standard Bible prints at that verse and its footnotes.

- Left out: a passage that says a word, name or number of the Bible as the BSB prints it is a mistake, a copyist's error, corrupt, interpolated or to be amended; or that a book was not written by the author its title or opening verse names; or that a biblical account is a myth.
- Kept: textual remarks that agree with what the BSB prints or leaves out (most of JFB's "the oldest manuscripts omit" remarks do); remarks on how the Hebrew or Greek can be read, or that a translation (the Septuagint, the Vulgate, the King James wording) is wrong; opinions the text does not settle; an apparent difficulty that the note harmonises; and errors, corruptions and fables of people, not of Scripture.

Each passage left out is the smallest that makes the claim: one sentence where the paragraph stands without it, or only the words themselves where the sentence stands without them. A note that opens with the words it explains ("12-15. there came a writing to him from Elijah the prophet--") keeps them. Each is in force at once with the line "A passage is left out here until a decision on it is made." (rule 5), for Shubhanshu to make final.

## Left out (14 passages in 14 notes)

- 2 Samuel 23:8 (sentence): the number is called corrupt and should be three hundred; the BSB prints eight hundred.
- 1 Chronicles 18:4 (sentence): it prefers seven hundred charioteers; the BSB prints seven thousand here and at 2 Samuel 8:4.
- 1 Chronicles 19:18 (sentence): it offers, as one option, that a number is corrupt (the BSB prints seven thousand here and seven hundred at 2 Samuel 10:18); it also gives a harmonisation, which stays. Unsure.
- 2 Chronicles 8:10 (sentence): one of the numbers (250 here, 550 at 1 Kings 9:23, both printed by the BSB) is called corrupt.
- 2 Chronicles 8:18 (sentence): one of the numbers (450 talents here, 420 at 1 Kings 9:28) is called corrupt.
- 2 Chronicles 15:8 (paragraph): the words "of Oded the prophet" are called a corruption to be erased; the BSB prints the prophecy of Azariah son of Oded the prophet.
- 2 Chronicles 21:12 (sentence): Elijah is called a copyist's error for Elisha; the BSB prints a letter from Elijah the prophet.
- Joshua 8:15 (sentence): "or Bethel" (8:17, printed by the BSB with no footnote) is presented as an interpolation missing from the oldest copies. Unsure.
- Isaiah 41:24 (sentence): the Hebrew text is called corrupt here, though the sense JFB accepts is the one the BSB gives ("your work is of no value"). Unsure.
- Jeremiah 15:14 (sentence): the reading the BSB prints ("I will enslave you to your enemies") is called needless and doubtless interpolated.
- Jeremiah 25:38 (words): "; probably interpolated from Jr 46:16", said of "sword", which the BSB prints ("the sword of the oppressor"); the rest of the note explains the sense the BSB gives and stays. Unsure.
- Philippians 4:13 (sentence): "Christ" is said to have been inserted by transcribers; the BSB prints "through Christ who gives me strength".
- Revelation 15:6 (sentence): the text should read "a stone" and drop "and"; the BSB prints "dressed in clean and bright linen".
- Revelation 18:3 (sentence): "the wine" may have been interpolated; the BSB prints "the wine of the passion of her immorality". Unsure.

## Kept after a closer look

- Deuteronomy 3:11 and 10:6: JFB rejects the view that the words are an interpolation and keeps them as the BSB prints them.
- Judges 19:10: "which is Jerusalem" is called an explanatory note by Ezra or a later hand; the note does not call the words wrong or say they should go, and the BSB prints them as a parenthesis.
- Isaiah 9:3: a remark on the written and the read Hebrew; no BSB word is called a mistake.
- John 8:10: some manuscripts lack the account, but JFB defends it as genuine, as the BSB prints it.
- Romans 9:5: JFB defends "God over all" as the BSB prints it and rejects the conjecture that drops "God".
- Romans 11:2 and 14:5: remarks on the King James wording and the Greek behind it, which the BSB's text agrees with.
- Ephesians 5:30: the oldest manuscripts omit "of His flesh and of His bones"; the BSB omits them too (its footnote gives them as the Byzantine and Received Text reading).
- 1 John 5:7: the BSB does not print the longer verse, and JFB agrees it is not genuine.

The other 339 flagged paragraphs were plainly within the kept list above: most are "the oldest manuscripts omit" remarks the BSB agrees with, and the rest are about people (false teachers, the heathen's fables, a sinner's error) or are harmonisations.
